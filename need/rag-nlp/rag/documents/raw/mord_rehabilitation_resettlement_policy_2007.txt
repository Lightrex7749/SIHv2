[SECTION: Objectives of the National Rehabilitation and Resettlement Policy, 2007]
The objectives of the National Rehabilitation and Resettlement Policy, 2007 are: to minimise displacement and to promote, as far as possible, non-displacing or least-displacing alternatives; to ensure adequate rehabilitation packages and expeditious implementation of the rehabilitation process with the active participation of affected families; to ensure that special care is taken for protecting the rights of the weaker sections of society, especially members of the Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes, and to create obligations on the State for their treatment with concern and sensitivity; to provide a better standard of living by making concerted efforts towards providing sustainable income to affected families; to integrate rehabilitation concerns into the development planning and implementation process; and, where displacement is on account of land acquisition, to facilitate a harmonious relationship between the requiring body and the affected families through mutual cooperation.

[SECTION: Legal Basis and Notification]
The National Rehabilitation and Resettlement Policy, 2007 (NRRP-2007) was formulated by the Department of Land Resources, Ministry of Rural Development, Government of India, and came into force with its publication in the Gazette of India on 31 October 2007. The policy has been circulated to various States and Union Territories for implementation, and its provisions provide for the basic minimum requirements that all projects leading to involuntary displacement must address; State Governments, public sector undertakings and other requiring bodies are free to offer benefit levels greater than those prescribed in the Policy.

[SECTION: Scope of Application]
The principles of the Policy apply to the rehabilitation and resettlement of families involuntarily and permanently displaced, whether the displacement arises from land acquisition or from any other reason. The Policy covers all cases of involuntary displacement and introduced a Social Impact Assessment (SIA) requirement for displacement affecting large numbers of families (historically referenced thresholds of the order of 400 families in plain areas and 200 families in tribal, hilly or Scheduled Areas), reflecting an intent that larger-scale, more consequential displacement receive more rigorous upfront assessment.

[SECTION: Rehabilitation and Resettlement Benefits]
The main categories of benefits available to affected families under the Policy include, where possible, land for land as compensation; skill development support and preference in project-related jobs (subject to availability of vacancies and suitability of the affected person), generally interpreted as at least one person per nuclear family; a rehabilitation grant in lieu of land or a job; an option for shares in companies implementing the project; and housing benefits for affected families. For linear acquisitions such as roads or railway lines, an ex-gratia payment in addition to compensation has been applied in practice, rather than the full package designed for large-scale, area-based displacement.

[SECTION: Institutional Mechanisms under the Policy]
The Policy provides for the appointment of an Administrator and a Commissioner for Rehabilitation and Resettlement, along with their powers and functions; the preparation of a Rehabilitation and Resettlement Plan for affected areas; the specification of Rehabilitation and Resettlement Benefits for Affected Families; a grievance redressal mechanism for affected persons; and a monitoring mechanism to track implementation of resettlement commitments over time.

[SECTION: Relevance to Disaster-Induced Relocation]
Although the National Rehabilitation and Resettlement Policy, 2007 was primarily framed around displacement caused by land acquisition for development projects, its stated principles — minimising displacement, ensuring adequate and expeditious rehabilitation, protecting the rights of weaker sections including Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes, and integrating rehabilitation concerns into planning — are the reference framework commonly cited when grounding disaster-driven relocation and resettlement planning in existing national policy, since India does not have a separate national resettlement policy specific to disaster-induced relocation.
